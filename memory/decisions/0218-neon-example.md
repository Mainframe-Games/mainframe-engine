# ADR 0218 — Neon: a second showcase, built with the engine features it needed

- **Date:** 2026-10-28
- **Status:** accepted
- **Docs:** docs/design/neon.md

## Context

The Forest shows daylight, terrain, foliage and water. Nothing showed the engine at night: emissive neon carried by
glow, dense instanced geometry, many small lights in rooms, in-world screens, interaction beyond one door, and a
living city. The request was a cyberpunk apartment about 20 floors up that the player can walk round but never leave:
two bedrooms, doors, a running shower, a computer room, and a balcony over traffic and towers.

Three engine gaps stood in the way: nothing followed a curve, a sub-viewport could not be shown on a 3D surface, and
point/spot lights over the shader limits were dropped in the order they were added.

## Decision

- **A new example project, `Examples/Neon` ("Neon Demo")**, shaped like the Forest: a standalone game project against
  this checkout (not in `MainframeEngine.slnx`), art built by seeded headless Blender scripts (the apartment, the city
  kit), CC0 ambientCG textures fetched by a script, original posters and fictional brands, all sound synthesised in C#
  (the music through adaptive music, ADR 0196), scenes generated by `--write-scenes`, released as
  `MainframeEngine.Neon-vX.Y.Z.zip` and listed in the editor's Download Example menu.
- **The engine gaps were built, not worked around**, each with its own ADR: paths (ADR 0215), viewport textures on 3D
  materials (ADR 0216) and nearest-light selection (ADR 0217). Neon is their first user: the train, airships and
  balcony fly-bys ride `PathFollow3D`s; six monitors and the TV are sub-viewport textures; the apartment has 16 room
  lights.
- **The city has no lights.** All its neon is emission, glow and haze; crowds of vehicles and towers are `MultiMesh`es
  driven by baked curves; close vehicles are path followers carrying sounds.
- **Interactables share small bases** (`Appliance` for on/off things, `DoorBase` for doors) and raise events the audio
  subscribes to; the computer's terminal drives the apartment through an `ITerminalHost`, so it is unit-tested without a
  GPU.
- **Light probes over the apartment** give sky occlusion indoors. Moving meshes are `GIMode.Disabled` so the committed
  bake stays current.

## Consequences

- Every frame allocates 0 B (gated by tests with the shower, TV, blinds, traffic and audio running); the Neon tests run
  without a GPU and skip the art tests on LFS pointers.
- Known limits: particles have no collision (streams die at a computed height); bus effects cannot change live, so
  indoor/outdoor audio is crossfaded loops plus per-voice distance low-passes; the towers are simple prisms whose detail
  is in the facade shader; the pause menu is the engine's drop-in `SettingsMenu` (volumes, rebinding, display), with no
graphics options of Neon's own.
