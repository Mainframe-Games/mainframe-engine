# ADR 0166 — Temporal anti-aliasing

- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** accepted (implemented on `forest/t`, forest slice wave 5)
- **Milestone:** Gameplay toolkit G8d.8 (TAA), with G8e.5's dithered alpha and room for G8e.8's TAAU
- **Spec:** docs/design/future/forest-showcase.md (G8d.8), docs/design/future/forest-visual-quality.md (G8e.5, G8e.8);
  current state in docs/design/post-processing.md (TAA), color-pipeline.md, project-and-gamehost.md

## Context

Brogan asked for TAA because leaves and grass shimmer under FXAA: FXAA smooths geometric edges in one frame but cannot
fix sub-pixel detail (needles, grass blades, leaf-card cut edges), which crawls as the camera moves. ADR 0163 built the
foundation: the post stages, the depth prepass with motion vectors (camera, moving nodes, foliage wind at both times,
the sky), the Halton (2, 3) jitter and ping-pong histories. Water has no motion vectors of its own (blended, not
prepassed) and its flow animates over a static bed.

## Decision

- **`AntiAliasing.Taa`** next to `None` and `Fxaa` (one enum: TAA replaces FXAA), through `rendering.antiAliasing`,
  `EngineOptions.AntiAliasing` and `IVulkanContext.AntiAliasing`. Default `None`: no existing frame changes.
- **`TaaEffect`**, a `BeforeTonemap` effect first in the stage (`Needs = Velocity | Jitter`), so auto exposure, glow,
  light shafts and the tonemap read the resolved, jitter-free image (`CopyToSceneColor`). One fullscreen pass:
  - reconstruct the current colour at the pixel centre from the 3 × 3 jittered samples, weights `exp(−2.29 d² / 0.75²)`
    (written in input pixels with an output/input ratio, so G8e.8's TAAU only changes the input size and the weights);
  - reproject with the closest-depth velocity of the 3 × 3; read the history with a 5-tap Catmull–Rom;
  - drop it on disocclusion: the history's alpha stores its view depth; it is kept only when some texel of the 3 × 3
    around the reprojected position saw this pixel's last-frame depth (± 15 %);
  - clip it to the YCoCg variance box (Salvi, γ = 1.25, inside min/max);
  - blend in Karis' luminance-weighted space (`c / (1 + exposure · luma)`) with feedback 0.94 still → 0.88 at
    8 px/frame → 0.2 on reactive pixels;
  - without history (first frame, cuts, resize, disocclusion) show a softer 3 × 3 reconstruction.
- **History resets** on the first frame, resize (the pool), a frame without a resolve, a view without motion history, and
  camera cuts: `RenderServer.ResetTemporalHistory()` (public; forgets every view's motion history too) and a change of
  the root world's camera, detected by the render server.
- **Sharpen: `TaaSharpenEffect`**, RCAS (FSR 1, MIT, re-implemented) as an `AfterTonemap` effect at
  `PostEffectOrder.Sharpen`, after the history and under the UI. Godot has no TAA sharpness, so the setting is named
  for what it does: `rendering.taaSharpness` / `EngineOptions.TaaSharpness` / `IVulkanContext.TaaSharpness`, 0–1,
  default 0.25 (0 disables the pass). G8e.8 keeps RCAS as TAAU's sharpen.
- **Water: a reactive mask, not a separate pass.** Water in the main scene pass blends with `BlendMode.AlphaReactive`
  (`a = dst · (1 − src.a)`); every opaque shader and the sky write alpha 1, so the resolve reads `1 − scene alpha` as
  reactivity. Drawing water after TAA would have meant a pass of its own over the resolved image (it is part of the
  scene pass: fog, depth test against the bed). With a full history the stream's ripples smeared along the flow; with
  the mask they are as crisp as under FXAA. Sub-viewports keep the plain alpha blend.
- **Dithered cutouts: `FoliageMaterial3D.AlphaDither`** (default off; on for `TreeMaterials`' leaves and the Forest's
  ferns). Under TAA (the frame is jittered) the cutout keeps a fragment when `(alpha − cutoff) / fwidth(alpha) + 0.5`
  beats interleaved gradient noise shifted per jitter sample; the history averages it into smooth coverage. One function
  (`include/alpha_dither.slang`) in the prepass and the foliage shader; the flag rides in `material.pbr.w`. Without TAA
  or without the flag it is the plain alpha test, so every existing golden is unchanged. G8e.5's
  `AlphaAntialiasingMode.AlphaToCoverage` can map onto it.
- **The Forest uses TAA.**

## Measurements (Apple M5, MoltenVK)

- Against a 4× supersampled frame (box-filtered in linear light): thin bars and sub-pixel spokes, mean error 1.20 with
  TAA vs 3.44 without AA (0.35 ×); swaying dithered leaves 3.11 vs 3.97 (0.78 ×).
- Tuning on the same scenes and on autowalk frame grabs: Unreal's wide `exp(−2.29 d²)` reconstruction blurred the
  converged image (1.51); feedback 0.9 tracked moving leaves a little better but flickered on still thin lines (0.29 vs
  0.17 mean change per frame); without the depth test a near swaying branch left a translucent copy of itself.
- `just forest-bench`, 1920 × 1080, on a machine shared with other lanes' GPU jobs: FXAA p50 14.03 / p99 23.29 ms (the
  quietest run), TAA p50 13.12 / p99 21.64 ms; eight interleaved runs spread 13–28 ms with no consistent difference.
  TAA's prepass saves about what the resolve, copy and sharpen cost. 0 B per frame.

## Consequences

- TAA softens the image slightly in motion; the sharpen restores part of it. Water aliases a little more than the rest.
- The scene colour's alpha now means something in the main view (1 − reactivity) until TAA runs, and TAA's view depth
  after it (`CopyToSceneColor` copies the history's alpha). Nothing downstream reads either.
- Particles (G6.3), Spine and animated transparent materials are not reactive yet and may smear under TAA; they can
  blend with `AlphaReactive` the same way.
- The Karis weighting uses the project exposure, not auto exposure's adapted value.
- TAAU (G8e.8) slots into the resolve: render-size inputs, Blackman–Harris weights by sub-pixel distance, a longer
  Halton cycle, mip bias.
